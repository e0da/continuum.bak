# Retained embedded mechanical transactions

`OwnedRuntimeMechanicalMethod.EmbeddedMidpoint` is a separate numerical strategy for the builtin owned model. Step-doubling remains available and retains its existing admission. A trial-safe HMR marker alone does not expose a derivative/force-stage contract: embedded mode rejects every nonbuiltin schedule before work, schedule replacement or restored-host installation.

The embedded driver retains source, midpoint, lower and accepted arrays per physical region. One hosted candidate bank owns each attempted interval. The original ordered actuator/resource fold runs once, producing terminal resource amounts, wheel momentum and mean local actuator intent. Two read-only samples evaluate wrench/environment at source and predicted midpoint poses. Sampling never withdraws resources, writes providers, appends thermal history or publishes a bank. On acceptance, terminal motion/provider state and midpoint thermal exposure publish once through the existing hosted transaction. Rejected attempts restore physical delivered-work counters and cancel the candidate; effort/row counters remain charged. Read pressure retains demand through the existing worker/timeline path.

The lower estimate freezes source wrench and uses ballistic kinematics. The upper uses midpoint wrench. Its position correction uses the initial and midpoint accelerations with weights `1/6` and `1/3`; constant acceleration remains exact instead of being penalized by an Euler-position comparator. Velocity differences are ordinarily of order `h²`, so interval adaptation uses exponent `1/2` and rechecks the unchanged position, velocity, angle and scaled angular-momentum budgets. The rotation comparator uses a frozen angular acceleration derived from total chassis-plus-wheel momentum; the accepted rotation uses the existing coupled midpoint solver. Thus asymmetric free-spin curvature is visible without a third wrench sample. These are local estimates, not global trajectory, energy or stiffness certificates.

Within the original limiter's held-control interval, the original resource fold fixes terminal quantities. Midpoint aggregate properties interpolate mass, first moment and full tensor about a common craft frame, expressed as a positive two-assembly combination about the midpoint COM to avoid subtracting huge origin tensors. Source-to-midpoint-to-terminal property maps retain the declared material-velocity/angular-velocity rule for expelled resource mass. This is an open-system model; it does not assert closed-body momentum or energy conservation during depletion.

For constant thrust and linear mass depletion, velocity and position use the exact inverse-mass integrals. Small mass fractions use convergent series to avoid log/cancellation loss. Gravity remains acceleration rather than a force divided through changing mass. Wheel state is withdrawn/updated once; total world angular momentum is independently checked in the closed constant-mass wheel fixture. Scalar resource/provider endpoint evolution is shared by both estimates because it is determined by the original held-input fold independently of mechanical pose in the current builtin model. A future pose-dependent resource/provider law needs an additional stage contract; it cannot inherit this admission.

The source, midpoint and terminal poses pass the session's read-only numerical spherical guard. Adjacent one-ULP clock tails use one source sample and the unchanged full mechanical/resource/individual-provider change bound, recording coalescence rather than claiming a midpoint sample. Queued input/event endpoints remain hard limits. Thermal forcing is inserted once at the midpoint; the independent thermal solver retains its own clock and budgets. The mechanical pair does not estimate midpoint environmental/heat quadrature error, and the thermal solver controls integration of its supplied forcing ledger rather than that quadrature. Actual atmospheric heat differences must be reported separately. Current temperature does not feed back into the admitted mechanical model.

Policy/checkpoint continuation stores primitive `method` (`0` step-doubling, `1` embedded). Runtime/binary version 5 has explicit earlier version maps owned by the codec. `AdaptiveObservation.EmbeddedAttempts` records attempted hosted operators and `EmbeddedForceSamples` records actual wrench evaluations. For this method `trialExecutions` counts attempted whole operators, not stage evaluations. Complete host clocks include limiter forecasting, retained setup, copies, mass interpolation, both samples, rejection, thermal completion and publication. Optional integration phase clocks bracket region math; there are no per-row timers. Existing step-doubling Systems phase counters accumulate accepted half-folds only, excluding coarse/rejected trial phases; its complete Adaptive WorkTicks includes all work. Embedded work/row counters retain attempts, while physical delivered ledgers roll back on rejection. A lower copy/force count is a source fact, not a measured speedup.

The default minimum interval remains 1e-4 seconds. A domain may explicitly admit
finer positive refinement without enlarging any error budget; policy ordering,
representable clock progress, bounded attempts and atomic rejection still apply.
The high-curvature circular-orbit check independently proves that the unchanged
coarse floor rejects, while finer refinement passes the same analytic reference.
The authored fast-reentry situation selects 1e-6 seconds rather than lowering its
speed or accepting an inaccurate interval.

Run `dotnet run --project tests/KspContinuum.EmbeddedMechanics.Tests/KspContinuum.EmbeddedMechanics.Tests.csproj -c Release`. Checks use independent constant-force/depletion integrals, wheel total momentum, asymmetric free-spin energy, circular-orbit convergence, nonzero-time clock tails, source-state/counter rollback and narrow schedule admission. Integration owns actual execution, the acquired-craft strategy comparison, complete costs and native consumer evidence.

The numerical rationale follows shared-stage explicit integration and embedded error indicators described by [Hairer and Lubich](https://www.unige.ch/~hairer/preprints/pcam-ode.pdf). Geometric rotation variables require a separate treatment; [Holzinger, Arnold and Gerstmayr](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11044-024-09970-8) discuss Lie-group integration for rigid multibody systems. Those references motivate the design, not a proof of this implementation or its claimed supported domain.

## Constant-property BS3(2) operator

`HigherOrderRK23` selects a separate Bogacki–Shampine 3(2) operator for the builtin stage contract. One candidate bank owns the original scalar fold. Four read-only wrench samples evaluate source, half, three-quarter and third-order endpoint states. The accepted weights are `2/9, 1/3, 4/9`; the second-order comparator uses `7/24, 1/4, 1/3, 1/8`. The comparator measures an order-three local difference, so the controller uses exponent `1/3` under the existing frozen budgets. This remains a local indicator, not a certified bound on the accepted trajectory's global error or stiff dynamics.

The state equations advance COM position/velocity, world angular momentum and quaternion orientation together. Quaternion derivative is one-half the product of world angular velocity and orientation. Raw quaternion stage components are retained for the derivative algebra; only the geometry view and final orientation are normalized. Normalizing the quaternion stage algebra itself would discard radial terms and can reduce the order, even for constant spin. World angular momentum responds directly to sampled world torque. The asymmetric free-body oracle uses independent body-frame Euler equations and an independent RK4 quaternion integration; central gravity is checked against circular motion and a step-size convergence sequence. Off-center thrust provides an independent constant-axis torque check.

The initial supported domain requires unchanged aggregate mass, COM and full inertia through the scalar transaction, plus zero source and terminal momentum in every individual admitted wheel reservoir. It admits aerodynamic torque, asymmetric free spin and held body-frame actuator forces in that domain. A mass-property change or internal rotor produces a visible `HigherOrderFallbacks` increment and reason, then cancels the preliminary bank and executes the existing midpoint method. This preserves the original ordered resource allocator and variable-mass/rotor model rather than freezing them. Preliminary attempts and all bulk sample work remain charged. Adjacent clocks without four distinct stages and session-stage domain rejection also fall back explicitly; ordinary unknown failures still cancel and propagate. Canceled banks never publish resources, input history or delivered-work ledgers.

The existing source/half/endpoint numerical sphere guard remains active, and the three-quarter sample also admits its actual finite-size clearance. These checks do not establish an arbitrary-force trajectory bound. Event/input and resource-frontier caps remain outside this operator. A rejected error estimate cancels its entire scalar/thermal intent and retries from the original bank.

Thermal publication occurs once. Aerodynamic heat uses the positive third-order stage weights, while ambient/convection exposure is the half-stage descriptor. This is an explicit forcing-quadrature treatment. Neither the mechanical pair nor the independent thermal solver certifies the error introduced by that forcing quadrature. Acquired atmosphere comparisons must retain temperature/energy differences separately from the mechanical budgets.

The observation now reports normalized `PositionError`, `VelocityError`, `AngleError`, `MomentumError`, `ScalarError` and `DominantError` for the last trial. Higher-order attempts, force samples and fallback counts/reasons distinguish actual execution from selection. The midpoint path reports the same component breakdown; its accepted arithmetic and budgets are unchanged. Source acceptance and analytic checks are separate from acquired workload accuracy, complete cost and native consumer qualification.

### Preflight exclusions

Higher-order admission now checks the current retained source before creating a candidate bank. Existing individual wheel momentum, a nonzero enabled wheel request, or a possible enabled propulsive mass withdrawal dispatches directly to midpoint with a `preflight-...` reason. Controls, enabled state and the same part/body/control-axis composition are read from the source-bound builtin model. This is deliberately conservative: starvation, electrical gating or a floating-point no-op can make a request physically inactive, but that does not turn a conservative fallback into an accepted RK23 execution.

A simple single-resource request whose original quantity subtraction is unchanged may proceed to the dynamic guard. No allocation is committed by this calculation. Mixture/shared-route cases retain conservative fallback; the original allocator is never reconstructed or replaced by admission. The actual post-fold property and individual-wheel checks remain authoritative if supply or any other scalar dependency defeats the preflight. Such a provisional trial cancels and its effort is still charged. `HigherOrderAttempts=0` with a preflight fallback distinguishes direct dispatch from canceled preparation; no extra numerical trial is counted merely for reading admission inputs. Held-input/native force and deadline semantics are unchanged.
